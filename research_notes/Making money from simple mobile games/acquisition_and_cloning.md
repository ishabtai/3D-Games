# User Acquisition Realities and the "Copy a Proven Pattern" Question (2024-2026)

Method note: Many primary sources (liftoff.ai, businessofapps.com, appmagic.rocks, supersonic.com, mobilegamer.biz, fkks.com, support.google.com, vectraplay.com, investgame.net PDFs) were blocked by the network egress proxy, so several figures below come from search-result summaries of those pages rather than full-page reads. They are marked "(snippet)". Apple's App Review Guidelines were read in full. Treat snippet numbers as indicative and check them before quoting exact figures.

## 1. User acquisition: CPI, ATT, organic discovery, ASO, short-form video, realistic organic downloads

### Takeaway
Paid installs for casual and puzzle games cost roughly $1-3+ on iOS in tier-1 markets and much less on Android or outside the US. iOS costs rose after ATT and keep rising. Only about 10% of new apps get any real traction, so a solo developer with no UA budget should expect very low organic volume unless a short-form video goes viral or the game gets featured.

### Cited Findings
**CPI benchmarks**
- Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report (data Feb 2024-Feb 2025): the average CPI for casual games was $1.41 on iOS and $0.14 on Android (snippet). The Android number is unusually low and may be a blended global figure heavy on low-cost markets. Verify it before relying on it. — [Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/); [Liftoff highlights](https://liftoff.ai/blog/highlights-2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/)
- Puzzle games average about $3.00 CPI on iOS and $2.00 on Android. Another summary says iOS puzzle players cost about 5x Android players (snippet, aggregator). — [Mapendo, Mobile Games CPI 2025](https://mapendo.co/blog/mobile-games-cpi-2025); [FoxData 2026 UA benchmarks](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/)
- Tier-1 game CPI averages in 2026: about $4.22 on iOS and $2.97 on Android. iOS usually runs 30-50% above Android (snippet, consultancy blog). — [Game Growth Advisor, CPI Benchmarks 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/)
- CPIs on Meta and TikTok rose about 15-20% year over year going into 2025 (snippet, aggregator). — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/)
- Hypercasual average CPI in 2025 was $1.50 on Android and $2.50 on iOS, against an estimated LTV of $2.80 (snippet, from a Gamesforum Intelligence report). — [Gamesforum Intelligence Hypercasual Report (Jul 2025)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Gamesforum-Intelligence-Hypercasual-Gaming-Report.pdf)
- Statista tracks global gaming CPI by genre and platform for 2025. The underlying figures are paywalled. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1241651/global-cpi-gaming-apps-genre-platform/)

**Effects of ATT**
- Summaries of 2025-2026 data put ATT opt-in at about 25-35% overall. Gaming is among the highest categories at about 39%, and sports games reach about 50% (Adjust data). One aggregator also quoted "9.1% in Q2 2025", which conflicts with the other figures and is probably a different metric. — [Adjust, ATT opt-in rates 2025](https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025/); [AppsFlyer, ATT opt-in higher than anticipated](https://www.appsflyer.com/blog/trends-insights/att-opt-in-rates-higher/)
- iOS CPI rose 19% year over year while iOS ARPU grew only 7%. More than 70% of iOS attribution decisions now rely on SKAdNetwork or probabilistic data (snippet, secondary blog). — [SignalSeal, ATT five years on](https://signalseal.net/blog/att-five-years-on); [Singular Q4 2025 report](https://www.singular.net/quarterly-report-2025-q4/)

**Organic discovery odds**
- More than 1.4M apps were released on Google Play and the App Store in 2025, and only about 10% "attracted any user attention" (snippet). — [MobileAction, organic app growth](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/organic-app-growth-in-2025/)
- The App Store received 557K new app submissions in 2025, up 24% from 2024. Games made up 72% of new releases, up from 63% in 2024 (snippet). — [Appfigures, Dec 2025](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251205?f=2)
- Apps that rely only on keyword search reportedly get about 70% fewer impressions than in 2020 (snippet, unverified secondary claim). — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/organic-app-growth-in-2025/)
- Some new apps have reached the top of the App Store through TikTok and Instagram creator partnerships and UGC (snippet). — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/organic-app-growth-in-2025/)

**Short-form video (TikTok / YouTube Shorts)**
- TikTok first shows a video to a small test audience and widens distribution if engagement holds. Developers who succeeded report that player and creator content outperformed the studio's own posts, and that 15-30 second "nothing to something" dev-progress clips work well. — [presskit.gg TikTok field guide](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/tiktok-indie-game-marketing)
- Conference talk on TikTok for indie games from IMPACT! Indie Games 2025 (Abi Le Guilcher, Future Friends). — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kz20_6MgBss)

### Inferences
- A Block Blast-style game with US iOS installs at about $2-4 needs LTV above that to scale. Ad-monetized puzzle LTV in tier-1 is usually close to CPI, which is why UA without a strong retention or ads edge loses money.
- Without paid UA, the realistic organic channels are (a) short-form video virality, which works like a lottery ticket, (b) featuring, and (c) ASO in long-tail keywords. Given the 10% traction figure, the base case for a solo developer is hundreds to low thousands of lifetime downloads. This is an inference; I found no rigorous study of solo-developer download distributions.
- Android and tier-2/3 geos are the cheap testing ground. iOS US is where revenue per user sits.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full Liftoff, AppsFlyer, or Adjust CPI tables because the domains were blocked. US-only puzzle CPI split by iOS and Android was not verified from a primary source.
- I found no rigorous data on typical organic downloads for solo developers, and no 2024-2026 postmortems with concrete TikTok-driven install numbers for casual or puzzle games.
- ASO basics were not researched in depth. The standard levers are title/subtitle keywords, icon and screenshot A/B tests (Google Play store listing experiments, Apple Product Page Optimization), ratings, and localization, but I have no cited source for them here.

## 2. Publishers (Voodoo, Homa, SayGames, Supersonic, Kwalee, CrazyLabs, Rollic, Azur)

### Takeaway
Publishers run CPI and retention tests on cheap prototypes and drop almost everything. Voodoo reportedly takes about 0.4% of about 1,000 annual prototypes to full launch. Rough prototype gates are sub-$0.30-0.50 CPI with D1 around 35-40%+ and D7 around 10%+. Revenue share terms are private and negotiated.

### Cited Findings
- Voodoo tests about 1,000 prototypes a year (doubling to about 2,000). Only about 0.4%, roughly four games a year, reach full launch, and about one becomes a hit (snippet). — [Deconstructor of Fun, How Voodoo builds hits (Jun 2025)](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/06/02/how-voodoo-builds-hits-four-product-lessons-from-a-2000-prototypes-a-year-machine)
- Voodoo's developer platform terms reserve Voodoo's right to negotiate exclusive cooperation or publishing agreements, including prototype development agreements, with developers negotiating in good faith within 60 business days (snippet). — [Voodoo Terms – Developers](https://voodoo.io/terms-platform)
- Voodoo reports going from 0 to $250M in hybridcasual revenue in 3 years (title). — [Voodoo news](https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-s-secret-sauce-from-0-to-250m-hybridcasual-revenue-in-3-years)
- Kwalee, Voodoo, and Homa run self-serve publishing platforms where you upload prototypes and get performance data (snippet). — [AppSamurai publishers list](https://appsamurai.com/blog/mobile-game-publishers-list/)
- Threshold benchmarks: about 40% D1 and 10% D7 with CPI of $0.30-0.50 before publishers scale. SayGames wants D1 of 35%+ plus a clear meta concept. Tilting Point asks for D7 of 20%+ and D30 of 8%+ with ARPU data (snippet, aggregator). — [Gamesforum Intelligence Hypercasual Report](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Gamesforum-Intelligence-Hypercasual-Gaming-Report.pdf); [Genzopia hypercasual stats 2026](https://www.genzopia.com/blog/hyper-casual-games-statistics-2026)
- A "kill rule" framework used in the industry: kill hypercasual prototypes that fail CPI < $0.30 and D1 > 35% within 72 hours of a soft-launch test. — [Bruin use case](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/hypercasual-soft-launch-kill-decision-framework/)
- Supersonic (Unity): 6.2B downloads in 5 years and about 190M MAU (Jan-Oct 2024). Its case study "Draw the Line" cut CPI from $0.80 to $0.21. It cites a 2024 hybrid title with D1 of 50%+. Unity engaged an advisor to divest Supersonic in March 2026. — [Supersonic 6.2B downloads](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/supersonic-from-unity-celebrates-6-2-billion-downloads-in-5-years/); [Draw the Line case study](https://supersonic.com/learn/case-studies/draw-the-line/); [Supersonic 2025 lessons](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/unveiling-2025s-top-lessons-for-hybrid-games/); [Unity Q1 2026 supplemental](https://s205.q4cdn.com/200788659/files/doc_presentations/2026/Mar/26/First-Quarter-Supplemental-Information.pdf)
- Homa passed 2B portfolio downloads in 2025. Kwalee has more than 1B downloads. SayGames has 119 hypercasual titles and more than 6B lifetime downloads (16% India, 11% US) (snippets). — [Outlook Respawn top hypercasual publishers Q3 2025](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-guides/top-10-hypercasual-gaming-publishers-worldwide-q3-2025); [Gamesforum report](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Gamesforum-Intelligence-Hypercasual-Gaming-Report.pdf)
- OLDER DATA (2022): Tenjin and GameAnalytics hypercasual CPI and retention benchmarks. — [Tenjin Q4 2022](https://tenjin.com/blog/hyper-casual-games-cpi-ad-spend-retention-benchmark-q4-2022/)

### Inferences
- The industry has moved from pure hypercasual to hybridcasual, and publishers now want a meta layer and IAP potential (SayGames' "clear meta concept", Supersonic's IAP-split examples). A bare mechanic clone is less likely to pass than it was in 2019-2021.
- For a solo developer, a publisher CPI test is a cheap way to validate an idea, since the publisher often pays for test UA. The odds of getting published are well under 1-5% per submission.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue share percentages for any of the named publishers. They are negotiated privately. Anecdotal "profit share after UA recoup" structures exist, but I found no citable source.
- No specific published thresholds for Homa, CrazyLabs, Rollic, Azur, or Kwalee. No acceptance rates beyond Voodoo's.

## 3. Cloning: legality, store policies, saturation, how followers differentiated

### Takeaway
In the US, game rules and mechanics are not copyrightable, but copying the specific look and feel (piece shapes and colors, board dimensions, art, names, icons) is infringement. Tetris v. Xio (2012) and Spry Fox v. 6waves (2012) are the key precedents. Apple 4.1/4.3 and Google's impersonation policy add store-level risk. Block puzzle is extremely crowded (1,000+ new titles in H1 2025 alone) but still growing. Winners like Block Blast and Royal Match won through execution, A/B testing, meta and characters, and heavy UA, not novelty of mechanic.

### Cited Findings
**Case law**
- Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive (D.N.J., May 30, 2012) granted summary judgment for Tetris against the iPhone game "Mino." Xio admitted it cloned Tetris but claimed it copied only unprotectable elements. The court found the visual expression "akin to literal copying" and that Mino copied the overall look and feel. — [Court opinion (govinfo PDF)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njd-3_09-cv-06115/pdf/USCOURTS-njd-3_09-cv-06115-0.pdf); [Loeb & Loeb analysis](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2012/06/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.)
- In the same case, the court also found trade dress infringement. It rejected Xio's argument that the color and style of the pieces were functional, calling them "arbitrary flourishes" unrelated to how the game works. — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2012/06/19/developments-in-the-tetris-vs-mino-legal-battle-could-provide-ammo-for-cloning-victims/); [Studicata brief](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/tetris-holding-llc-v-xio-interactive-inc)
- Spry Fox v. Lolapps/6waves (W.D. Wash., 2012), Triple Town vs. Yeti Town: the court refused to dismiss and rejected 6waves' argument that copyright never protects game design against clones. 6waves then settled (Oct 2012), transferred the Yeti Town IP to Spry Fox, and kept the terms confidential. — [TouchArcade on initial ruling](https://toucharcade.com/2012/10/02/triple-town-court-makes-initial-ruling-offers-a-new-interpretation-on-cloning/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/spry-fox-6waves-settle-suit-over-alleged-i-triple-town-i-clone); [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/045709/6waves-settles-with-spry-fox-in-yeti-town-triple-town-clone-debate/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spry_Fox,_LLC_v._Lolapps,_Inc.)
- Scholarly consensus drawn from these cases: general game ideas, mechanics, and scènes à faire are not protected, only their specific expression. — [Wikipedia: Video game clone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_clone); [Federal Lawyer, Oct/Nov 2012](https://www.fedbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/ipinsight-octnov2012-pdf-1.pdf); [Nissenbaum Law](https://www.gdnlaw.com/blog/internet-law/tetris-game-protected-copyright/)
- Related older precedents: Capcom v. Data East (1994) went against the plaintiff, with fighting-game mechanics found unprotectable. King (Candy Crush) also litigated over "Saga" naming, which is a trademark route rather than copyright. — [Wikipedia: Capcom v. Data East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom_U.S.A._Inc._v._Data_East_Corp.); [Courthouse News: "Saga" games creator](https://www.courthousenews.com/cant-do-that-saga-games-creator-says/)

**Store policies (Apple read in full)**
- Apple 4.1(a): "Don't simply copy the latest popular app on the App Store, or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI and pass it off as your own." 4.1(b): impersonation violates the Developer Code of Conduct and may lead to removal from the Developer Program. 4.1(c): no use of another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your icon or name without approval. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple 4.3(b): "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available." Saturated categories are rejected unless they offer "a meaningfully different or improved experience." 5.2.1 bans copycat names and metadata and unlicensed trademarks or copyrighted works. Apple runs an IP dispute web form. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Google Play Impersonation policy bans misleading users about an app's connection to another developer or app through icons, titles, descriptions, or in-app elements. Its examples include copying a famous TV show's character and title in the icon (snippet). — [Google Play Impersonation](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en); [Impersonation FAQs](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16341334?hl=en)

**Saturation**
- In H1 2025, more than 2,500 new games launched across Block Puzzle, Sort, and Screw Puzzle, with 1,000+ each in Sort and Block Puzzle. Even so, Block Puzzle downloads grew 71% in 2025 (Sort +14%, Screw +27%), making it the fastest-growing casual subgenre in revenue and downloads (snippet). — [AppMagic Casual Report H1 2025](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-h1-2025); [AppMagic Casual Report 2025](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-2025)
- Mobile puzzle games made more than $10B in IAP in 2025 (+14% YoY) from more than 9.7B downloads (snippet). — [Sensor Tower via gamedevreports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-state-of-the-mobile)

**How followers differentiated**
- Block Blast! (Hungry Studio) had about 216M installs in 2024, second only to Roblox in AppMagic's 2024 downloads chart, and 40M DAU / 160M MAU by Dec 2024. It later reached 70M DAU / 300M MAU and was #1 worldwide by downloads in Jan-Mar 2026. Growth is credited to relentless A/B testing, heavy UA, and giving up short-term ROI for long-term retention. The game shares the 1010!/Woodoku drag-and-clear core mechanic. — [Gamesforum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/how-block-blast-shattered-records-with-ai-a-b-testing-and-a-bold-bet-on-long-term-players); [Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/block-blast-maker-hungry-studio-a-b-tested-its-way-to-the-top-of-the-charts/); [BusinessWire Apr 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423998721/en/Block-Blast-Ends-Q1-2026-as-the-No.-1-Most-Downloaded-Mobile-Game-Worldwide); [BusinessWire Mar 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260312302688/en/Hungry-Studios-Block-Blast-Reinforces-Its-Position-Among-the-Free-Mobile-Games-Players-Turn-to-Most)
- Royal Match (Dream Games, 2021) passed Candy Crush Saga in revenue from July 2023. It hit $1B about 2 years after launch, $2B by early 2024, and about $3B 7 months later; user spending passed $6B by May 2025. Differentiators: the King Robert character and a narrative "help someone" frame, strong level design, about 200 new levels a month of LiveOps, and a paid-UA-heavy mix (61.5% of installs paid vs 15-25% for Candy Crush, snippet). — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/royal-match-surpasses-candy-crush-saga-in-revenue-and-downloads-for-the); [Naavik deep dive](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/royal-match/); [gaming.news](https://gaming.news/news/2025-05-20/user-spending-in-mobile-puzzle-game-royal-match-surpasses-6-billion/); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/2b-revenues-royal-match-dethrones-candy-crush)

### Inferences
- Legally safe pattern: copy the rules (drag pieces onto an 8x8 grid, clear lines) but make all expression original (art, piece style, palette, UI layout, sounds, name, icon). Avoid confusingly similar names like "Block Blast 2" and look-alike icons. The Tetris case shows that copying visual identity, not the mechanic, is what loses lawsuits.
- The bigger risk is commercial, not legal. With 1,000+ block puzzles launched per half-year, a clone with no UA budget and no retention edge is invisible. Apple 4.3(b) can also reject near-identical submissions.
- Both big followers (Block Blast, Royal Match) beat incumbents on execution plus heavy paid UA, which a solo developer can't match. A solo developer's realistic differentiation is a niche theme, a twist mechanic, or a meta layer rather than out-spending anyone.

### Gaps
- Non-US law (EU, China) on game clones was not researched.
- There is no public, detailed account of how 4.3(b) is enforced against puzzle clones specifically (for example, rejection rates).
- Woodoku's (Tripledot) and 1010!'s download histories were not retrieved.

## 4. Does a known character or IP help in casual games?

### Takeaway
An original character can be a strong differentiator (Royal Match's King Robert). Licensed IP is not a guaranteed win: some licensed casual titles perform modestly, and licensing is expensive and practically unavailable to solo developers. Using IP without a license breaks Apple 5.2.1 and Google's impersonation policy.

### Cited Findings
- Royal Match's original character King Robert is credited as the key differentiator from Candy Crush's generic candy theme, and Dream Games extended him into the sequel Royal Kingdom. — [Growth Hacking Lab case study](https://thegrowthhackinglab.com/case-studies/dream-games-81-million-dollar/); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/royal-match/)
- Licensed casual games vary widely. Sensor Tower's UK Q3 2025 "entertainment mascot" charts show Disney Magic Match 3D (Jam City) peaking at about $8.5K weekly iOS revenue in the UK, while Hello Kitty Nail Salon (Budge) was near $90 per week with falling downloads (783 per week by late Sept). — [Sensor Tower UK iOS Q3 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q3-ios-top-5-entertainment%20mascot%20games-units-gb-60cb61082829329604f8b61d); [Sensor Tower UK Android Q3 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q3-android-top-5-entertainment%20mascot%20games-units-gb-60cb61082829329604f8b61d)
- Big IP holders run casual games through major publishers (for example, Scopely holds Marvel, Wizarding World, Hello Kitty, and Hasbro properties). — [Naavik, Scopely 2026](https://naavik.co/digest/dissecting-scopelys-mobile-empire/)
- Unauthorized use of third-party trademarks or characters is prohibited (Apple 5.2.1). Google specifically cites copying a TV show's character in an icon as impersonation. — [Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play Impersonation](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer, the practical version of "IP helps" is creating an original mascot or theme that makes the game recognizable and gives ad creatives a hook, not licensing famous IP. Public-domain characters or folklore themes are a possible low-cost middle path. This is an inference and was not researched.

### Gaps
- No quantitative 2024-2026 study comparing licensed and original IP casual games on CPI or retention.
- No data on licensing costs or minimum guarantees for small developers.
